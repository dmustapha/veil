## Scene 1 — Hook (`hook`)
> Keep your ETH on Ethereum. Get working USDC on Stellar. Veil moves the proof, never the collateral, and reveals neither your amount nor your wallet.

## Scene 2 — Problem 1: capital mobility (`capital`)
> Your ETH sits on Ethereum. The Circle USDC you want runs on Stellar's fast, low-cost rails. Reaching it today means bridging your ETH, or selling it, and giving up the position you meant to keep.

## Scene 3 — Problem 2: privacy (`privacy`)
> So you borrow against your ETH instead of selling. But on a public chain, borrowing leaks everything: your exact collateral, your loan size, and the wallet behind it. For real size, that is a permanent, public target.

## Scene 4 — Solution statement (`solution`)
> Veil gives you both. Lock real ETH on Ethereum. Borrow real USDC on Stellar. The collateral never bridges. Only a commitment, an amount, and a zero-knowledge proof ever cross between the chains.

## Scene 5 — The flow (`flow`)
> Here is the path. You escrow real WETH on Ethereum, bound to a private commitment. Axelar carries only that commitment and its amount to Stellar. In your browser, you generate a Groth16 proof that your hidden collateral clears the threshold. Soroban verifies it natively, and releases real Circle USDC. Your ETH never left Ethereum.

## Scene 6 — The guarantee (`guarantee`)
> No relayer, no committee decides your loan. A Groth16 proof, verified on-chain, is the only thing Stellar trusts. It is built on your device, so your amount and your identity never leave the browser. Forge it, and verification fails before a single dollar moves.

## Scene 7 — Live now / proof (`proof`)
> And this runs today. Real WETH locked on Ethereum. The attestation delivered to Stellar through Axelar. Around six real Circle USDC borrowed against a zero-knowledge proof. Both provers verify live, on-chain, and a tampered proof is rejected.

## Scene 8 — Close (`close`)
> Veil. Your collateral stays yours, on Ethereum. Your privacy stays yours, on Stellar. Move the proof, not the money.
